    Module 3 Resources: Market Dynamics and Labor Markets¶

    Interactive Simulations (McCreary)¶

    These browser-based simulations let you explore Module 3 concepts hands-on. All simulations are from Dan McCreary's Introduction to Economics (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0).

    Simulation What It Teaches Try It
    Supply and Demand Shift supply and demand curves to see how equilibrium price and quantity change Open
    Market Equilibrium Explorer Set prices above or below equilibrium and observe surplus or shortage forming Open
    Market Structure Spectrum Compare output, pricing, and consumer surplus across perfect competition, oligopoly, and monopoly Open
    Price Ceiling Simulator Set a price ceiling below equilibrium and watch a shortage develop — then consider rent control or fuel price caps Open
    Price Floor Visualizer Set a minimum wage above equilibrium and explore the trade-off between higher wages and potential unemployment Open
    Labor Force Breakdown Explore how the labor force divides by sector, formality status, and gender — with data parallels to India's PLFS Open
    Unemployment Classifier Read real-world scenarios and classify each as frictional, structural, cyclical, or seasonal unemployment Open

    Textbook Readings¶

    Book Relevant Chapters / Sections
    Chang, H.-J. (2014). Economics: The User's Guide Ch. 3: How Economics Developed (markets and the price system)
    Wheelan, C. Naked Economics Ch. 1: The Power of Markets; Ch. 3: Government and the Economy (market failure)
    Varoufakis, Y. Talking to My Daughter About the Economy Ch. 3: Markets and finance
    Krugman, P. (1994). Peddling Prosperity Ch. 9: The economics of trade and geography
    Krugman, P., Wells, R. & Graddy, K. Essentials of Economics Ch. 3: Supply and Demand; Ch. 4: Price Controls and Taxes; Ch. 7: Market Structure
    Frank, R. H. (2011). The Economic Naturalist Examples on pricing, competition, and labor markets
    Staley, S. R. (2018). Contemporary Film and Economics Ch. on financial crisis films (relevant to Margin Call)
    Langlois, R. N. Economics Through Film (UConn) It's a Wonderful Life unit: financial intermediation, bank runs; The Founder unit: entrepreneurship, labor

    Videos¶

    1. "How Supply and Demand Set Prices" — Marginal Revolution University, ~10 min. Clear visual explanation of the supply-demand model with real-world examples.
    2. Paul Krugman's Nobel Prize Lecture — nobelprize.org, ~45 min. Krugman explains new trade theory and economic geography in his own words.
    3. "Margin Call — Economics Explained" — YouTube, ~15 min. Walks through the economic mechanisms depicted in the film, including fire sales and liquidity crises.
    4. "India's Gig Economy: Zomato, Swiggy, and the Future of Work" — YouTube documentaries exploring labor market conditions for platform workers in India.
    5. "What is an Oligopoly?" — Marginal Revolution University, ~8 min. Explains strategic interdependence among firms with examples from the telecom and airline industries.
