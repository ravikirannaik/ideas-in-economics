    Module 3 Quiz: Market Dynamics and Labor Markets¶

    Test your understanding of the key concepts from Module 3. Each question has one correct answer. Click "Show Answer" to check your response.

    1. When the price of onions rises sharply due to a poor monsoon, households switch to potatoes and reduce their onion consumption. Which economic concept best explains this response?¶

    1. Supply shift
    2. The law of demand (substitution effect)
    3. Market failure
    4. Price floor
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The law of demand predicts that when the price of a good rises, the quantity demanded falls. One mechanism driving this is the substitution effect: consumers switch to relatively cheaper alternatives (potatoes instead of onions). A supply shift (A) is what caused the price increase — the poor monsoon shifted the onion supply curve leftward — but the consumer response of switching to substitutes is explained by the law of demand.

    Concept Tested: Law of Demand / Substitution Effect

    2. After Reliance Jio entered India's telecom market in 2016 offering free calls and nearly free data, mobile data prices crashed across the industry. What happened in the market for mobile data?¶

    1. The demand curve shifted leftward
    2. The supply curve shifted rightward due to a new seller, reducing equilibrium price
    3. The market experienced a shortage
    4. The government imposed a price ceiling
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The entry of Jio dramatically increased the supply of mobile data services. This rightward shift of the supply curve — with more data available at every price level — pushed the equilibrium price downward. This was a market-driven price reduction, not a government price ceiling (D). Demand did not decrease (A); if anything, lower prices increased the quantity demanded along the demand curve.

    Concept Tested: Supply Shift / Market Equilibrium

    3. The Indian government sets a Minimum Support Price (MSP) for wheat above the market equilibrium price. What is the likely result?¶

    1. A shortage of wheat
    2. An increase in demand for wheat
    3. A surplus of wheat, as quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded
    4. No effect, since MSP is just a recommendation
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. A price floor set above the equilibrium price causes a surplus. At the higher MSP, farmers are incentivized to produce more wheat (quantity supplied rises), while buyers purchase less at the higher price (quantity demanded falls). The gap between quantity supplied and quantity demanded is the surplus, which the government must purchase and store. India's Food Corporation of India (FCI) holds millions of tonnes of grain precisely because of this mechanism. A shortage (A) would occur if the price were set below equilibrium.

    Concept Tested: Price Floor / Surplus

    4. Demand for salt is highly inelastic, while demand for a specific brand of basmati rice is highly elastic. Which combination of factors best explains this difference?¶

    1. Salt is cheaper; basmati rice is more expensive
    2. Salt has few substitutes and is a small share of the budget; branded basmati rice has many substitutes
    3. Salt is produced domestically; basmati rice is imported
    4. Salt is a necessity; basmati rice is a luxury
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Price elasticity of demand depends heavily on the availability of substitutes and the share of the good in the consumer's budget. Salt has virtually no substitutes and takes up a tiny fraction of household spending, making demand highly inelastic. A specific brand of basmati rice competes with many other brands and types of rice, giving consumers easy alternatives — making demand for that particular brand highly elastic. While (D) captures part of the story (necessity vs. luxury), the most precise explanation centers on substitutability and budget share.

    Concept Tested: Elasticity of Demand

    5. India's telecom market has consolidated from dozens of operators in the 2000s to three dominant firms (Jio, Airtel, Vodafone Idea) by 2024. This market structure is best described as:¶

    1. Perfect competition
    2. Monopoly
    3. Monopolistic competition
    4. Oligopoly
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. An oligopoly is a market dominated by a few large firms whose pricing and output decisions are strategically interdependent — each firm must consider how its rivals will react to its choices. India's telecom sector, with three firms controlling over 90% of subscribers, fits this definition precisely. Perfect competition (A) requires many small firms with no market power. Monopoly (B) requires a single firm. Monopolistic competition (C) requires many firms with differentiated products.

    Concept Tested: Market Structures / Oligopoly

    6. A fresh engineering graduate from IIT Bombay turns down two job offers to continue searching for a position that better matches her specialization in machine learning. She is currently experiencing:¶

    1. Structural unemployment
    2. Cyclical unemployment
    3. Frictional unemployment
    4. Seasonal unemployment
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Frictional unemployment arises from the normal process of job search — workers voluntarily transitioning between jobs or searching for a better match. The graduate has job offers available but is choosing to continue searching for a better fit. This is generally short-term and can be economically beneficial, as it improves the match between workers and jobs. Structural unemployment (A) would apply if her skills were genuinely obsolete or mismatched with available jobs. Cyclical (B) is tied to economic downturns. Seasonal (D) relates to predictable periodic fluctuations.

    Concept Tested: Types of Unemployment

    7. In Margin Call, the firm decides to sell its entire portfolio of toxic mortgage-backed securities in a single trading day, knowing this will crash the price. In supply-demand terms, what is happening?¶

    1. The demand curve for these securities shifts rightward
    2. The supply curve shifts massively rightward, driving the price far below the previous equilibrium
    3. The market reaches a more efficient equilibrium
    4. The government imposes a price floor on the securities
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The fire sale involves dumping a massive quantity of securities onto the market simultaneously. This is a dramatic rightward shift of the supply curve — hugely more securities are available for sale at every price level. With demand unchanged (or even falling as buyers grow suspicious), the equilibrium price collapses. This is not a move toward efficiency (C) — it is a crisis-driven distortion that imposes negative externalities on every other holder of similar securities.

    Concept Tested: Supply Shift / Fire Sale Dynamics

    8. Paul Krugman's new trade theory explains why Germany and France trade large volumes of cars with each other, even though both countries have similar technology and factor endowments. The key mechanism is:¶

    1. Comparative advantage based on differing natural resources
    2. Government subsidies to automobile manufacturers
    3. Increasing returns to scale combined with consumer preference for variety
    4. Lower labor costs in one country compared to the other
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Classical trade theory (based on comparative advantage from differing endowments, option A) cannot explain why similar countries trade similar goods. Krugman showed that when production exhibits increasing returns to scale, firms benefit from specializing in specific varieties. Germany specializes in BMWs, France in Peugeots. Both countries then trade, and consumers in both benefit from greater variety at lower per-unit cost. This intra-industry trade — similar countries trading similar products — is the dominant pattern in global trade and the phenomenon Krugman's theory was designed to explain.

    Concept Tested: New Trade Theory / Increasing Returns

    9. Bangalore accounts for over 35% of India's IT exports, despite having no particular natural resource advantage for software production. Krugman's economic geography explains this concentration through:¶

    1. Government mandates requiring IT firms to locate in Bangalore
    2. Agglomeration effects — skilled labor pools, knowledge spillovers, and specialized suppliers that make the cluster self-reinforcing
    3. Bangalore's lower cost of living compared to other Indian cities
    4. Comparative advantage from Bangalore's tropical climate
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Krugman's new economic geography explains industrial clusters through agglomeration (centripetal) forces: once a few IT firms established in Bangalore, they attracted a pool of skilled engineers, which attracted more firms, which attracted specialized training institutes and venture capital, which attracted more engineers. This self-reinforcing cycle — the hallmark of increasing returns in geographic space — explains why the cluster persists and grows even as costs (real estate, congestion) rise. The government did not mandate the concentration (A), and Bangalore is not the cheapest Indian city (C).

    Concept Tested: Economic Geography / Agglomeration

    10. During the Yes Bank crisis of 2020, depositors rushed to withdraw their savings after learning about the bank's exposure to bad loans. This is an example of:¶

    1. Seasonal unemployment affecting banking staff
    2. A supply-side shock to the credit market
    3. A liquidity crisis triggered by a loss of confidence, where the market for deposits fails to clear
    4. Efficient market adjustment to new information
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. A bank run is a classic liquidity crisis: depositors, fearing the bank cannot repay them, all attempt to withdraw simultaneously. Since banks lend out most of their deposits, they cannot honor all withdrawal requests at once. The market for bank deposits breaks down — not because of a fundamental inability to repay over time, but because of a coordination failure driven by loss of confidence. This is a form of market failure, not an efficient adjustment (D). The RBI's intervention — imposing a moratorium and arranging a rescue — was necessary precisely because the market's self-correcting mechanisms were amplifying the crisis rather than resolving it.

    Concept Tested: Liquidity Crisis / Market Failure
