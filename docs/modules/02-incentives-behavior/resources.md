## Interactive Simulations (McCreary)

These browser-based simulations let you explore Module 2 concepts hands-on. All simulations are from Dan McCreary's [Introduction to Economics](https://dmccreary.github.io/economics-course/) (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0).

| Simulation | What It Teaches | Try It |
|---|---|---|
| Demand Curve Explorer | Shift demand and supply to see how market equilibrium responds --- the aggregate result of strategic choices by buyers and sellers | [:material-open-in-new: Open](https://dmccreary.github.io/economics-course/sims/demand-curve-explorer/) |
| Marginal Utility Meter | See how satisfaction diminishes with each additional unit --- the rational basis for individual decision-making | [:material-open-in-new: Open](https://dmccreary.github.io/economics-course/sims/marginal-utility-meter/) |
| Budget Constraint Explorer | Move along the budget line to visualize trade-offs between two goods --- the choice set each player brings to a game | [:material-open-in-new: Open](https://dmccreary.github.io/economics-course/sims/budget-constraint-explorer/) |
| Elasticity Calculator | Test how responsive buyers and sellers are to price changes --- essential for understanding pricing strategies | [:material-open-in-new: Open](https://dmccreary.github.io/economics-course/sims/elasticity-calculator/) |
| Diminishing Marginal Benefit | Watch how the benefit of each additional unit declines --- the logic behind strategic decisions about "how much" | [:material-open-in-new: Open](https://dmccreary.github.io/economics-course/sims/marginal-benefit-pizza/) |

## Videos

1. **"Nash Equilibrium --- Explained Simply"** --- YouTube (Economics Explained), ~12 min. Clear walkthrough of Nash equilibrium using everyday examples.
2. **"The Prisoner's Dilemma"** --- YouTube (Veritasium), ~15 min. An engaging exploration of why cooperation is hard and how it can be sustained.
3. **John Nash's Nobel Prize Lecture** --- [nobelprize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/1994/nash/lecture/), ~45 min. Nash explains his work on non-cooperative games and equilibrium in his own words.
4. **"Game Theory in Real Life"** --- Marginal Revolution University, ~10 min. Applications of game theory to auctions, bargaining, and strategic competition.
5. **"What is Information Asymmetry?"** --- Khan Academy, ~8 min. Clear introduction to adverse selection and moral hazard with accessible examples.
