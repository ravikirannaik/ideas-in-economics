# Module 5 Quiz: Innovation, Technology, and Growth

#### 1. India's GDP is approximately five times larger than Switzerland's, yet Switzerland's GDP per capita is roughly thirty times higher. What does this tell us?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. India is a poorer country than Switzerland in every respect
2. GDP per capita is a better indicator of average living standards than total GDP
3. Switzerland has a more efficient government than India
4. India's GDP statistics are unreliable
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Total GDP measures the size of an economy, but GDP per capita — GDP divided by the population — captures average living standards. India's large GDP reflects its enormous population; when spread across 1.4 billion people, the per-person share is much lower than Switzerland's. A country can be simultaneously an economic giant (large GDP) and relatively poor on a per-person basis (low GDP per capita).

    **Concept Tested:** GDP vs. GDP per Capita

#### 2. According to the Solow growth model, what happens when a country keeps adding capital (machines, factories) while holding the number of workers and the level of technology constant?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Output per worker grows at a constant rate forever
2. Output per worker eventually stops growing because of diminishing returns
3. Output per worker declines because workers cannot operate so many machines
4. The economy enters a recession
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Diminishing returns to capital is the central mechanism of the Solow model. The first machine added to a workplace dramatically raises output. The fiftieth machine, when there are only twenty workers, adds almost nothing. Eventually, the additional output from new capital barely covers the replacement of worn-out capital, and the economy reaches a steady state where output per worker stops growing. Only technological progress can overcome this barrier.

    **Concept Tested:** Solow Model / Diminishing Returns

#### 3. Robert Solow's 1957 study found that capital accumulation explained only about 12% of American economic growth from 1909 to 1949. The remaining 88% — the "Solow residual" — represents:

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Government spending on infrastructure
2. Population growth
3. Total factor productivity — technology, knowledge, management, and institutional improvements
4. Foreign investment in the American economy
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. The Solow residual, or total factor productivity (TFP), captures everything that makes an economy more efficient beyond simply having more machines and workers. This includes technological innovation, better management practices, improved institutions, education, and the diffusion of knowledge. The striking size of the residual — 88% — is what makes technology the central concern of growth economics.

    **Concept Tested:** Total Factor Productivity / Solow Residual

#### 4. When Reliance Jio entered Indian telecom in 2016 with free calls and ultra-cheap data, several established operators went bankrupt or merged. This is an example of:

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Market failure
2. Creative destruction
3. Diminishing returns
4. Network effects
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Creative destruction, a concept from Joseph Schumpeter, describes the process by which radical innovation displaces incumbent firms and industries. Jio's entry destroyed the old pricing structure and pushed weaker competitors out of the market, but it simultaneously created enormous value — cheaper data, wider connectivity, and a new ecosystem of digital services. The "destruction" of old firms and the "creation" of new economic activity are two sides of the same process.

    **Concept Tested:** Creative Destruction

#### 5. In *Moneyball*, Billy Beane's Oakland Athletics achieved 103 wins with a payroll of $40 million, while the New York Yankees won 103 games with $125 million. In economic terms, Oakland had higher:

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Total output
2. Capital stock
3. Productivity — more output (wins) per unit of input (payroll)
4. Market power
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. Productivity measures output per unit of input. Oakland produced the same number of wins (output) with roughly one-third of the payroll (input), meaning its productivity was approximately three times higher than the Yankees'. This mirrors the Solow model's insight: what matters for performance is not just how much you spend (capital accumulation) but how efficiently you use what you have (technology/TFP).

    **Concept Tested:** Productivity / Film Connection

#### 6. India's UPI payment system went from zero transactions in 2016 to over 14 billion per month by 2024. This explosive growth is primarily explained by:

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Government subsidies for digital payments
2. Network effects — the system became more valuable as more people and merchants joined
3. Diminishing returns to cash transactions
4. Creative destruction of the banking system
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. UPI exhibits classic network effects: as more merchants accepted UPI payments, more consumers had reason to adopt it; as more consumers used it, more merchants had incentive to accept it. This created a virtuous cycle that accelerated growth once a critical mass of users was reached. While government policy (including demonetization) played a supporting role, the fundamental driver of UPI's explosive adoption was the self-reinforcing dynamic of network effects.

    **Concept Tested:** Network Effects / Indian Context

#### 7. The concept of human capital refers to:

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. The financial wealth that individuals accumulate over their lifetime
2. The machines and equipment that workers use in production
3. The skills, knowledge, training, and health that make workers productive
4. The social connections that help people find employment
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. Human capital encompasses the education, skills, training, experience, and health that make workers productive. Like physical capital (machines and factories), human capital is an investment — students give up current income to acquire skills that raise their future productivity. Countries that invest heavily in education and health tend to grow faster because their workforces can generate, adopt, and apply new technologies more effectively.

    **Concept Tested:** Human Capital

#### 8. A platform like Swiggy connects restaurants (producers) and hungry customers (consumers), taking a cut of each transaction. As more restaurants join, the platform becomes more useful for customers, which attracts more customers, which attracts more restaurants. This describes:

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Diminishing returns to capital
2. A two-sided network effect
3. Capital deepening
4. The Solow residual
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. A two-sided network effect occurs when increasing participation on one side of a platform (restaurants) increases the value for the other side (customers), and vice versa. This positive feedback loop is the defining feature of platform economics and explains why successful platforms can grow explosively once they reach a tipping point. It also explains the winner-take-all dynamics that characterize many digital markets.

    **Concept Tested:** Platform Economy / Network Effects

#### 9. Robert Solow's model predicts that poor countries should grow faster than rich countries because they have more room to accumulate capital and adopt existing technologies. Which of the following best explains why India grew much faster after 1991 than before?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. India suddenly received large amounts of foreign aid
2. India's population growth accelerated, adding more workers
3. Liberalization allowed firms to adopt global technologies, enter competitive markets, and improve efficiency (raising TFP)
4. India discovered significant new natural resources
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. India's post-1991 growth acceleration was driven primarily by improvements in total factor productivity (TFP), not by increases in capital or labor alone. The dismantling of the License Raj, reduction of trade barriers, and opening to foreign investment allowed Indian firms to adopt global technologies, reorganize production more efficiently, and compete in world markets. The IT services revolution — built on organizational innovation and human capital rather than heavy capital investment — is the clearest example of TFP-driven growth.

    **Concept Tested:** Solow Model / TFP / Indian Context

#### 10. In *Moneyball*, Peter Brand — a Yale economics graduate — applies statistical methods to baseball player evaluation. After Oakland's success, every team adopts analytics, and Oakland's competitive advantage disappears. This illustrates the economic concept of:

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Diminishing returns — analytics stopped working as more teams used them
2. Moral hazard — teams took more risks with analytics
3. Diffusion of innovation — once technology proves its value, competitors adopt it and the advantage spreads
4. Market failure — the analytics market was inefficient
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. The diffusion of innovation describes how new technologies and methods spread from early adopters to the rest of an industry or economy. Oakland's advantage was temporary because the innovation (sabermetrics) was not proprietary — it was based on publicly available statistical methods. Once other teams saw it work, they adopted it, often combining it with larger budgets. This mirrors the Solow model's prediction that technology eventually diffuses across economies, raising productivity for everyone who adopts it — but eliminating the first-mover's temporary advantage.

    **Concept Tested:** Diffusion of Innovation / Film Connection
