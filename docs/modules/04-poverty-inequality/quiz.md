# Module 4 Quiz: Poverty, Inequality, and Development

#### 1. What is the key difference between absolute poverty and relative poverty?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Absolute poverty is measured in dollars; relative poverty is measured in rupees
2. Absolute poverty uses a fixed threshold; relative poverty is defined in relation to the median standard of living
3. Absolute poverty applies only to developing countries; relative poverty applies to developed countries
4. Absolute poverty measures income; relative poverty measures consumption
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Absolute poverty measures deprivation against a fixed standard (e.g., the World Bank's $2.15/day line), while relative poverty defines being poor in relation to the society around you (e.g., the EU's threshold of 60% of median income). Both measures can be applied in any country (ruling out C), and both can use either income or consumption data (ruling out D). The currency is irrelevant — the distinction is between fixed vs. relative thresholds.

    **Concept Tested:** Absolute Poverty vs. Relative Poverty

#### 2. India's Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) measures deprivation across three dimensions. Which of the following is NOT one of them?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Health
2. Education
3. Income level
4. Living standards
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. The MPI measures deprivation in health (nutrition, child mortality), education (years of schooling, school attendance), and living standards (cooking fuel, sanitation, water, electricity, housing, assets). Income level is deliberately excluded — the entire point of the MPI is to capture dimensions of poverty that income alone misses. A family above the income poverty line may still be multidimensionally poor if they lack sanitation, clean cooking fuel, or adequate schooling.

    **Concept Tested:** Multidimensional Poverty Index

#### 3. A country has a Gini coefficient of 0.60. This indicates:

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Very low inequality — income is distributed nearly equally
2. Moderate inequality
3. High inequality — income is concentrated among a relatively small share of the population
4. The country is very poor
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. The Gini coefficient ranges from 0 (perfect equality) to 1 (one person holds all income). A value of 0.60 indicates very high inequality — comparable to South Africa, one of the most unequal countries in the world. The Gini measures *distribution*, not *level* — a rich country can have a high Gini (like the US) and a poor country can have a lower one (like Bangladesh), so D is incorrect.

    **Concept Tested:** Gini Coefficient

#### 4. In *Slumdog Millionaire*, Jamal works as a chai server at a call center while the employees earn many times his wage. This illustrates which economic concept?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Comparative advantage
2. Labor market segmentation
3. The law of demand
4. Production possibilities frontier
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Labor market segmentation describes the division of the labor market into distinct tiers with different wages, conditions, and mobility prospects. The call center workers possess formal credentials (English fluency, tertiary education) that give them access to the globalized service economy. Jamal, despite his intelligence and multilingual ability, lacks these formal credentials and is confined to the informal tier. They work in the same building but inhabit different labor markets.

    **Concept Tested:** Informal Economy / Labor Market Segmentation

#### 5. Approximately what share of India's total employment is in the informal economy?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. About 25%
2. About 50%
3. About 65%
4. About 86%
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. According to the Periodic Labour Force Survey and National Statistical Office estimates, roughly 86% of India's total employment is informal — workers without written contracts, social security, or coverage under labor law. This means the informal economy is not a marginal or residual sector in India; it is the dominant mode of work. Even within formally registered enterprises, many workers are employed informally.

    **Concept Tested:** Informal Economy

#### 6. Banerjee and Duflo's RCT of microfinance in Hyderabad found that access to microcredit:

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Dramatically reduced poverty and empowered women, confirming the claims of microfinance advocates
2. Increased borrowing and business investment but had no significant effect on consumption, health, or women's empowerment
3. Was harmful to borrowers because of high interest rates
4. Had no effect whatsoever on any economic outcome
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. The Hyderabad RCT, published in the *American Economic Journal: Applied Economics* in 2015, found that microcredit access increased business investment and self-employment, but did not translate into measurable improvements in household consumption, education, health, or women's empowerment over the study period. Microfinance was neither the transformative revolution (A) nor worthless (D) — it was a useful financial service with modest economic effects, far short of the claims that had won Muhammad Yunus the Nobel Peace Prize.

    **Concept Tested:** Microfinance / RCTs

#### 7. Amartya Sen's capability approach defines development as:

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Maximizing GDP per capita
2. Reducing the Gini coefficient below 0.30
3. Expanding the freedoms people have to achieve valued functionings
4. Industrializing the economy as quickly as possible
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. Sen argued that development should be measured not by income (A) or inequality statistics (B) alone, but by what people are actually free to *do* and *be* — their capabilities. A person who earns above the poverty line but cannot read, or who has income but lacks the freedom to participate in political life, is deprived in ways income does not capture. This framework influenced the design of the Human Development Index, the MPI, and the Sustainable Development Goals.

    **Concept Tested:** Capability Approach

#### 8. India ranks as the world's fifth-largest economy by GDP, yet its Human Development Index (HDI) rank is approximately 134th. This discrepancy reveals that:

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. India's GDP statistics are unreliable
2. The HDI is a flawed measure that should be replaced
3. Aggregate economic output does not automatically translate into better health, education, and living standards for citizens
4. India's population is too large for GDP to be meaningful
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. India's GDP reflects total economic output, which is large because of its population and growth rate. But the HDI — which combines health (life expectancy), education (years of schooling), and income (per capita) — shows that this output has not been fully converted into human development outcomes for the average citizen. The gap between GDP rank and HDI rank is a powerful illustration of why economists like Amartya Sen and Mahbub ul Haq advocated for measures beyond GDP.

    **Concept Tested:** Human Development Index

#### 9. Pratham's "Teaching at the Right Level" approach, validated by Duflo and Banerjee's RCTs in India, improved learning outcomes by:

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Building new schools in every village
2. Providing laptops to every student
3. Grouping children by learning level rather than age and using targeted tutoring
4. Increasing teacher salaries by 50%
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. The intervention grouped children by their actual learning level — not their age or grade — and used volunteer tutors to bring struggling students up to basic reading and arithmetic competency. RCTs across multiple Indian states showed significant improvements in learning outcomes. The approach was effective precisely because it addressed the "schooling without learning" problem: many children were enrolled in school but not learning at grade level. The evidence led to adoption by several Indian state governments, reaching millions of children.

    **Concept Tested:** RCTs / Education Returns

#### 10. In *Slumdog Millionaire*, Latika and Jamal start in identical poverty, but their life trajectories diverge sharply. From the perspective of the capability approach, Latika's constrained trajectory illustrates:

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. That women are inherently less capable than men
2. That the informal economy does not employ women
3. That gender-based restrictions reduce women's capabilities even when their initial income is identical to men's
4. That poverty affects only men
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. The capability approach focuses on what people are *free* to do and become. Latika faces gender-specific constraints — restricted mobility, vulnerability to male control, limited access to even the informal labor markets that Jamal navigates — that reduce her capabilities far below Jamal's, despite starting from the same economic position. This illustrates a core insight of the capability approach: two people with identical incomes can have vastly different capabilities if one faces social constraints (such as gender norms) that the other does not.

    **Concept Tested:** Gender Gap / Capability Approach
