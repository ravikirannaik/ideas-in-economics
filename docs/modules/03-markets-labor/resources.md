## Interactive Simulations (McCreary)

These browser-based simulations let you explore Module 3 concepts hands-on. All simulations are from Dan McCreary's [Introduction to Economics](https://dmccreary.github.io/economics-course/) (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0).

| Simulation | What It Teaches | Try It |
|---|---|---|
| Supply and Demand | Shift supply and demand curves to see how equilibrium price and quantity change | [:material-open-in-new: Open](https://dmccreary.github.io/economics-course/sims/supply-and-demand/) |
| Market Equilibrium Explorer | Set prices above or below equilibrium and observe surplus or shortage forming | [:material-open-in-new: Open](https://dmccreary.github.io/economics-course/sims/market-equilibrium/) |
| Market Structure Spectrum | Compare output, pricing, and consumer surplus across perfect competition, oligopoly, and monopoly | [:material-open-in-new: Open](https://dmccreary.github.io/economics-course/sims/market-structure-spectrum/) |
| Price Ceiling Simulator | Set a price ceiling below equilibrium and watch a shortage develop — then consider rent control or fuel price caps | [:material-open-in-new: Open](https://dmccreary.github.io/economics-course/sims/price-ceiling/) |
| Price Floor Visualizer | Set a minimum wage above equilibrium and explore the trade-off between higher wages and potential unemployment | [:material-open-in-new: Open](https://dmccreary.github.io/economics-course/sims/price-floor/) |
| Labor Force Breakdown | Explore how the labor force divides by sector, formality status, and gender — with data parallels to India's PLFS | [:material-open-in-new: Open](https://dmccreary.github.io/economics-course/sims/labor-force/) |
| Unemployment Classifier | Read real-world scenarios and classify each as frictional, structural, cyclical, or seasonal unemployment | [:material-open-in-new: Open](https://dmccreary.github.io/economics-course/sims/unemployment-classifier/) |

## Videos

1. **"How Supply and Demand Set Prices"** — Marginal Revolution University, ~10 min. Clear visual explanation of the supply-demand model with real-world examples.
2. **Paul Krugman's Nobel Prize Lecture** — [nobelprize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2008/krugman/lecture/), ~45 min. Krugman explains new trade theory and economic geography in his own words.
3. **"Margin Call — Economics Explained"** — YouTube, ~15 min. Walks through the economic mechanisms depicted in the film, including fire sales and liquidity crises.
4. **"India's Gig Economy: Zomato, Swiggy, and the Future of Work"** — YouTube documentaries exploring labor market conditions for platform workers in India.
5. **"What is an Oligopoly?"** — Marginal Revolution University, ~8 min. Explains strategic interdependence among firms with examples from the telecom and airline industries.
